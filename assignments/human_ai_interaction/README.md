# Human–AI Interaction Assignment  
**Course:** AIPI 590 — Emerging Trends in Explainable AI  
**Instructor:** Dr. Brinnae Bent  
**Semester:** Fall 2025  

## Overview
For this assignment, I explored how humans interact with AI explanations and how to communicate complex XAI methods to different audiences.  
The goal was to take a single explainable or interpretable method and explain it in **two distinct ways**, each tailored to a specific audience.

This project helped me think critically about how explanation style, tone, and visuals affect understanding and trust in AI systems.

## Deliverables

### Explanation 1 – Blog for a General Audience
- **Topic:** SHAP (SHapley Additive Explanations)  
- **Format:** Blog (Google Sites)  
- **Audience:** General public  
- **Link:** [Human–AI Interaction Blog](https://sites.google.com/view/humanaiinteraction/home)  

**Summary:**  
In this blog, I explain SHAP using the idea of teamwork and fairness. Each feature in a model is treated like a player on a team, and SHAP helps determine how much each “player” contributed to the final prediction.  
My goal was to make interpretability approachable by combining simple language, analogies, and visuals. I focused on helping non-technical readers understand *why* SHAP matters and *how* it improves transparency and trust in AI.

### Explanation 2 – Visual Diagram for a Technical Audience
- **Topic:** LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations)  
- **Format:** Infographic (Canva Design)  
- **Audience:** Data-science students / AI practitioners  
- **Link:** [LIME Infographic on Canva](https://www.canva.com/design/DAG1y3pAS_I/x6LDEtgpiDwLwwwVssAdUQ/edit?utm_content=DAG1y3pAS_I&utm_campaign=designshare&utm_medium=link2&utm_source=sharebutton)  

**Summary:**  
This infographic breaks down how **LIME** explains predictions made by complex black-box models. Using the visual metaphor of a lime slice, the design emphasizes *locality* (zooming in on one prediction) and *interpretability* (building a simple surrogate model). The five sequential steps—select instance → perturb data → build local dataset → train interpretable model → interpret results—show how LIME approximates a model’s behavior near a specific example.  

The diagram uses concise text, color-coded steps, and visual icons to make the workflow intuitive while maintaining technical accuracy. It targets readers with some machine-learning background who want a clear conceptual overview without code.  

**Reflection:**  
Compared with my SHAP blog for a general audience, this visual explanation prioritizes *structure and precision* over narrative. While SHAP’s story-based analogy fostered accessibility and trust among non-technical readers, LIME’s infographic supports quick comprehension and recall for technical audiences, highlighting how explanation style and medium shape understanding and engagement.
